01 / BIOGRAPHY

About

Shuzhi Sam Ge, Fellows of SAEng, IEEE, IFAC, IET, ACA, AAIA, CAA and PEB. Professor, Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering. He received his Ph.D. and DIC from Imperial College London, U.K., in January and July 1993 respectively, and his B.Sc. degree from the Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Beijing, China, in 1986. His current research interests include intelligent control, artificial intelligence, robotics, and research translation.

He serves as President of the Asian Control Association (2024–2026), IFAC Council Member (2023–2026), Singapore Lead and Asian Co-lead of INPACE, and Founding Editor-in-Chief of the International Journal of Social Robotics. He previously served as Vice President for Technical Activites (2009-2010) and Membership Activities (2011-2012) of the IEEE Control Systems Society.

He has received major honours incl. Singapore National Technology Award, Inaugural Temasek Young Investigator Award on Intelligent Control of Unmanned Vehicles, IEEE CSS Distinguished Member Award, AI Singapore Grand Challenge Award in Robust AI. He has authored 10 monographs and 800+ publications, with 70,000+ citations and an h-index of 126 and was a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher for 10 consecutive years (2016–2025). He has led as (co-)PI or participated in 20+ research grants.

Industrial Robotics

Adaptive control for physical machines

Robot dynamics, neural approximation and adaptive control provide a foundation for precision manipulation under uncertainty. The research connects mathematical stability analysis with robotic manufacturing.

Research in practice

Mobile Robotics

Control, navigation and coordination

Moving beyond fixed workspaces brings sensing, motion and coordination into the same control problem. This research extends across mobile platforms and helicopter systems, including the modelling and control of coupled dynamics.

Social and Service Robotics

Robots that interact with people

Social and service robotics brings perception, communication and human interaction into robot design. Adam illustrates this move towards robots as participants in everyday human environments.

Research and community

Constrained control

Barrier Lyapunov functions for the control of output-constrained nonlinear systems ↗

Tee, K. P., Ge, S. S., & Tay, E. H.

Automatica, 45(4), 918–927.

Significance. Introduces Barrier Lyapunov Functions into control design to keep the output within prescribed limits. It connects constraint enforcement with Lyapunov-based stability analysis under the stated assumptions.

20051,907 citations
Switched systems

Switched linear systems: control and design ↗

Sun, Z., & Ge, S. S.

Springer London.

Significance. Provides a systematic framework for controllability, observability, stabilizing switching and feedback design. It connects structural analysis of switched systems with constructive control methods.

20131,412 citations
Stable neural control

Stable adaptive neural network control ↗

Ge, S. S., Hang, C. C., Lee, T. H., & Zhang, T.

Springer Science & Business Media. 2013 eBook.

Significance. Develops systematic adaptive neural-control designs with explicit analysis of stability and transient performance. It provides a foundation for using neural approximation in uncertain nonlinear control systems.

20111,157 citations
Input constraints

Adaptive tracking control of uncertain MIMO nonlinear systems with input constraints ↗

Chen, M., Ge, S. S., & Ren, B.

Automatica, 47(3), 452–465.

Significance. Addresses adaptive tracking when actuator inputs are constrained. An auxiliary design system accounts for input saturation within the controller design for uncertain MIMO systems.

20041,074 citations
MIMO nonlinear control

Adaptive neural control of uncertain MIMO nonlinear systems ↗

Ge, S. S., & Wang, C.

IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks, 15(3), 674–692.

Significance. Uses block-triangular structure to design adaptive neural controllers for coupled uncertain systems. The analysis establishes semiglobal uniform ultimate boundedness while avoiding controller singularities for the considered system classes.

2000905 citations
Adaptive backstepping

Adaptive neural network control for strict-feedback nonlinear systems using backstepping design ↗

Zhang, T., Ge, S. S., & Hang, C. C.

Automatica, 36(12), 1835–1846.

Significance. Combines neural approximation with recursive backstepping design for uncertain strict-feedback nonlinear systems, linking online adaptation with a systematic control-design procedure.

Adaptive neural network control of robotic manipulators ↗

Ge, S. S., Lee, T. H., & Harris, C. J.
